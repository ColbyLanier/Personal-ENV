### Following Instructions for Consistency ​

Sarah discussed the importance of following detailed instructions when working on a project, emphasizing the need to avoid repetitive tasks and ensure consistency. She explained the structure of a template they were using, highlighting the various tabs and fields, and the need to adhere to the existing values in the template to avoid errors during the upload or import process.

### Use of NIGP codes & separate content file ​

Wanda and Dawn contributed to the discussion, with Dawn confirming that they were using NIGP codes. Sarah also mentioned that they would be using a separate file for entering content, to avoid having to delete tabs from the original file.

### Creating Product and Item Codes ​

Sarah explained the process of creating product and item codes for a catalog. She emphasized that a product is a parent to its associated items, and each item is a variation of the product. For example, a product could be “jogger pants” with different sizes and colors as items. The product code is created by starting with a contract number, followed by a hyphen and a 3-digit character in sequence. Once a new product is identified, a new sequential number is used. Sarah also clarified that the manufacturer’s part number is specific to the product, while the supplier’s part number is specific to the item.

### Contract Item Identification and Categorization ​

Sarah explained the process of identifying and categorizing different items in a contract document, which could be in various formats such as a PDF or an Excel spreadsheet. She emphasized the importance of differentiating between items based on their product codes, descriptions, and warranty information.

### Cataloging Process and Documentation Methods ​

Donna highlighted her practice of highlighting issues and adding notes for future reference. Sarah emphasized the importance of documenting questions and issues in the Excel file for easy review. Dawn suggested using the comment feature in Smart Sheet for faster question resolution. Sarah also demonstrated how to use dynamic views and filters in Smart Sheet for contract analysis.

### Exploring AI Chat Bots for Data Extraction ​

Donna, Sarah, and Arayana discussed the challenges and potential solutions for extracting data from PDFs. They examined the use of AI chat bots for this purpose and the potential of Intelligent Character Recognition (ICR) to improve the process. Sarah noted a goal of building at least 250 catalogs by the end of the year.

### Creating Catalogs With Smart Sheet ​

Sarah guided the team through the process of creating a catalog using the Smart Sheet dashboard. She highlighted the need to update contract information in Smart Sheet, save the contract document to the G-drive, follow the naming scheme, and use the catalog item-entry template.

### Creating New Product Code for Sealants ​

Pavement markings, crack sealants, and joint sealants were treated as variations of one product. A new product code based on the contract number was created: product name “Pavement Crack/Joint Sealant,” commodity code 90911, supplier listed as manufacturer, summary description “Cracked or joint sealant,” and keywords such as “pavement,” “crack,” “joint,” and “sealant.”

### Improving Product Searchability in Catalog ​

The team brainstormed keywords—“concrete,” “asphalt,” “pavement,” “crack,” “sealant,” “PCC,” “construction,” “airport,” “maintenance,” “repair,” etc.—to enhance product searchability and agreed to pull additional specifics from bid documents if needed.

### Product Details Entry and Template Use ​

Sarah walked through entering product details into the catalog template for pavement-joint crack-sealing services, stressing accurate item names, codes, pricing, units of measure, and adherence to approved codes. She plans to add reference information such as tax codes and Incoterms to the guide.

### Cataloging and Tagging Contract Items ​

Item tags specific to the contract were used to identify catalog entries. Copy-and-paste techniques helped maintain consistency across product codes and descriptions, with clarifications (e.g., crack-filler width greater than ¾-inch).

### Preparing New Contract Catalogs and Training ​

Sarah is assigning product codes and colors to item groups, populating detailed descriptions, and saving the catalog file using contract number and expiration date. Once complete, the catalog is marked ready for quality review, and training videos plus assignments will follow.

### Dashboard Field Updates and Navigation ​

Guidance was provided on editing dashboard fields, grouping columns for easier navigation, and ensuring that blue-highlighted fields remain the editable set. The term “commodity code” may be renamed “material group” for clarity.

### Automating Document Status and Processes ​

Status automatically changes to “in progress” or “contract not on website” based on content-creator updates. Content creators should not edit status fields directly; automation rules handle state transitions.

### Assigning Tasks and Managing Contracts ​

Task filtering and assignment procedures were demonstrated, with expired contracts excluded from reports. Plans include a custom catalog for active contracts and support for multiple assignees.

### Task Assignment and Spreadsheet Updates ​

Tasks will be grouped by topic and assigned to specific team members. Spreadsheet updates (e.g., supplier numbers) will streamline workload distribution, and strategic assessment discussions with leadership will continue.
