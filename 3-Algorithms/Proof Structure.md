An algorithmic proof of correctness is a formal argument that necessitates correctness. Because the proofs are often formulaic, Inductive, Contradiction, etc there are reliable logical structures to force a tautology. 

# n
most proofs are arguments about n. The proof, and more broadly the algorithm it describes, should almost always be approached from the perspective of n rather than the baked perspective present in the problem.
